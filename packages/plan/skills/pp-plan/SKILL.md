  Keep this instance's plans -- plans/<slug>/ as documents whose structure, statuses and prose THIS script alone writes: every section of a node's body by `section` (a plan born by its Context, a phase or a batch prepared as a line of its parent's Items and born at its first section, the kind's order guided, a log appended, one removed by `--remove`), deduce every status from the writing (Expected frames, Delivery delivers and the closing rises to the plan, a framing section rewritten reopens; `done` checks a todo off, `block` says a stop), derive progress (`status`), lint all (`check`) and mend what is mechanical (`repair`); `move` and `rename` a node, `mount` its chain, `constrain` its laws and `serve` its readings, say each plan's `next-move`, answer the engine's `verify`. Use whenever a plan is created or advances; never open a plan document in an editor.

# `pp-plan` — the plans keeper

One writer, one structure: `plans/<slug>/PLAN.md`, one directory and `PHASE.md` per phase, one directory and `BATCH.md` per batch -- three levels, always. An ADDRESS is bare names, `memo/core/storage`, never the numbered folders: NAME is the identity, used by entries and addresses; the disk FOLDER is indexed, `<n>-<name>`, DERIVED from the position owned by `item.<n>` keys -- the script alone renames folders when the order changes, and every birth says its path from the repo root. Front matters carry one `item.<n>: <name>  <status>` per child (statuses `todo · doing · done · blocked`, plus `redo` reachable only by guarded `reopen`). The BODY is titled SECTIONS in the kind's order -- plan: Context, Survey, Target, Protocol, Expected, Arbitrations, Items, Decisions; phase: Context, Ground, Target, Expected, Arbitrations, Items, Decisions; batch: Context, Ground, Target, Expected, Tests, Arbitrations, Items, Decisions, Delivery -- each written by `section`: a standing section is REPLACED, an absent one INSERTED at its rank, a log (Decisions, Delivery) or the `--append` flag APPENDS, a name beyond the list lands at the tail. A subtitle inside a section is written `###`: a prose carrying a `## ` line, a code block included, refuses `prose-heading` before anything is written. `--remove[=<n>]` removes one section instead, heading and text, and reads no stdin: a name standing several times needs its rank (`section-ambiguous` lists each occurrence with its size and first line), a section of the kind's list standing once refuses `section-kept`, and no status moves. The order is GUIDED: a required section waits the required ones before it (`section-order`). An item lives in three moments: PREPARED, a line `- <slug> -- <brief>` in its parent's Items, the entry derived, no document -- amend the line freely until the item is framed; BORN, its first section (Ground) creates its folder and its document from the line, the brief as Context; DELIVERED, its statuses moved as the work lands. The plan alone is born by its Context. The STATUSES are deduced from the writing: Expected makes a node `doing`; Delivery makes a batch `done` (its todos all done, `items-open` otherwise) and the closing rises to the phase and the plan, each closing said; a framing section rewritten on a `done` node reopens it (`redo`, the ancestry rises); `block` says a stop, lifted by the next framing section; `done` checks a todo off -- no other verb moves a status, and no word closes a node. Every move is surgical: the one `status:` line and its parent entry.

    ./pp <key> -s pp-plan section <address> <Name> [--append]   (prose on stdin, `-` marks it)
    ./pp <key> -s pp-plan section <address> <Name> --remove[=<n>]   (no stdin)
    ./pp <key> -s pp-plan done <slug>/<phase>/<batch>/<todo>
    ./pp <key> -s pp-plan block <address> <reason ...>
    ./pp <key> -s pp-plan move <src-address> <dst-parent-address> <index>
    ./pp <key> -s pp-plan rename <address> <new-name>
    ./pp <key> -s pp-plan mount <address> [--whole]
    ./pp <key> -s pp-plan constrain <address> [<prefix>] --section production|behavior   (laws on stdin; a line opening on a posed code replaces it)
    ./pp <key> -s pp-plan constrain <address> --retire <code>
    ./pp <key> -s pp-plan serve <address> <token ...> | serve <address> -   (rows on stdin)
    ./pp <key> -s pp-plan next-move
    pp-plan.py verify   (the engine's call, never the agent's: a vector on stdin)
    ./pp <key> -s pp-plan status [<slug>]
    ./pp <key> -s pp-plan check
    ./pp <key> -s pp-plan repair

`move` transfers a phase to another plan, a batch to another phase, or reorders within its parent: the entry is written at `<index>`, both parents renumber, folders follow derived names, the moved document's model keys name its new home and its Items line follows it; a prepared item moves by its parent's Items alone. The call SAYS what remains yours: subtree prose, including references to the old home. The TITLE line of every document `move` and `rename` touch -- the node and the batches it carries -- follows in the same call: it becomes the title its kind's template gives its front matter, the rest of the body byte for byte; a free title, one not opening on `# PLAN`, `# PHASE` or `# BATCH`, is left. `rename` gives a phase or a batch a new name -- the parent's entry at its rank, its Items line, the derived folder, `slug:` and the batches' `phase:` follow in one call, every refusal before the first write; a plan keeps the name it was born under and a todo renames by its parent's Items; the prose that says the old name stays yours, and the call says so. A law amends BY ITS CODE: a `constrain` line opening on a code posed here replaces that law in place, a bare line derives a new code; `constrain <address> --retire <code>` takes the law out of `constraints` and keeps its code under `retired:` with the date and the text -- the writer's inventory reads it as taken, so a code never returns. `serve <address> <token ...>` appends ONE row to the last section of the node's `serve:` rows -- the readings its mount carries; `serve <address> -` replaces the whole block with the rows piped, a blank line separating two sections, and an empty block refuses `value-malformed`. A token is written in one of three forms, preferred in this order: the whole document (`DOC`); the lines between two tags (`DOC[start..end]`, either side may stay empty -- a tag may hold blanks, so quote the token on the command line; a piped row needs no quotes); line ranges (`DOC[x-y,...]`) as a last resort -- the answer names every line range it wrote, because the numbers stop pointing at the same lines when the file changes. A piped row with a bracket left open refuses `serve-range-malformed`, and a token the engine's writer refuses (`token-invalid`, `serve-range-malformed`) refuses here by the same name. A rework reopens the node carrying that component's history by a framing section rewritten (a new todo in Items, its Target), and ANCESTRY rises with it in the same call (closed phase → `redo`, closed plan → `doing`) so the rework stays visible to the plan's next move. `check` reddens any done node still carrying an engaged child, a line against its document, a broken numbering, a DERIVED folder (`<n>-<name>`) without its entry, a section name twice in one document, a level title that differs from its front matter or a missing title -- any other directory is the plan's own material and says nothing, and a prepared todo without a document is no finding. `repair` mends what is MECHANICAL -- the numbering, a line against its document (the document wins), a derived folder without its entry, a plan or a phase open over children all done (closed: the cascade that never ran), a level title set from its front matter or a missing one inserted -- and says every mend; every other finding is said with its gesture and left; played on the operator's GO.

The plans' nodes are VECTORS a reflex may project a frustration on -- three families this package declares at its manifest, each `verify: pp-plan`: `plan:<slug>`, `plan-phase:<slug>/<phase>`, `plan-lot:<slug>/<phase>/<batch>` -- the address every verb takes, the depth saying the grain. `verify` is the engine's call at every entry and service of the bus, never the agent's: the address must RESOLVE in the tree (a plan born; a phase or a batch born or PREPARED as an Items entry) -- `path-unknown` otherwise, `address-depth` when the depth does not fit the family, `family-foreign` for a family this package does not declare.

`mount` is the READ-ONLY working-view macro, playable ANY time: it derives the document chain to host from an address. The chain GROWS by level: two entries for a plan (package PLAN method nested with its body, plan instance stacked), four for a phase, six for a batch; a PREPARED item, or one not even prepared yet under a born parent, mounts its kind's method ahead -- three for a phase to come, five for a batch to come -- so the framing that births it plays under its laws, and the combined GO that frames a parent then a child mounts once. The LAST document of the chain -- the node the address names, or its parent when the node is still to come -- mounts its whole body; each ancestor above it mounts the sections that arm the level below, from its Target on: a plan shows Target and Expected, a phase Target, Expected and Arbitrations, each part served under `<title>[<start>..<end>]`; a section the ancestor lacks says nothing, and its laws, offers and `serve:` rows come as before. `mount <address> --whole` mounts every ancestor's whole body instead -- use it when a framing needs an ancestor's Ground or Items. It emits a `::mount <json>` directive pp collects, so one pp call mounts the whole chain; nothing is written and no status moves. After a mount every segment offers `pp-plan`: address the method's segment (`-s pp-plan@<n>`) so the framing runs under its governing laws. `next-move` is READ-ONLY too: it prints the next-move lines the bus holds, one per open plan, and pushes nothing.

Refusals are named -- `path-unknown` (an absent entry says Items, a numbered segment says the bare address, a prepared item says its first section), `section-unknown`, `section-order`, `item-line-malformed`, `item-engaged`, `items-open`, `done-invalid`, `slug-taken`, `name-invalid`, `kind-mismatch`, `code-unknown`, `prose-missing`, `prose-heading`, `section-ambiguous`, `section-kept`, `index-invalid`, `target-unknown`, `reopen-invalid`, `status-invalid`, `value-malformed`, `token-invalid`, `serve-range-malformed`, `frontmatter-malformed`, `argument-missing`, `argument-unexpected`, `verb-unknown`, `method-missing`, `not-an-instance` -- exit 2, nothing written; a malformed call says the verb's usage in one sentence. Writes are atomic. `status` is DERIVED from front matters alone, the prepared items shown; `check` confronts every line with its document, so hand edits cannot hide.
